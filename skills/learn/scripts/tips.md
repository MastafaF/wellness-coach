# Learning Tips
# Format: each tip is a single line starting with the category tag
# Topics: Deen (Islam), History, Space, Tech

## quran

[quran] Surah Al-Kahf: the Prophet (PBUH) said whoever reads it on Friday will have light between the two Fridays. (Sahih al-Jaami)
[quran] Ayat al-Kursi (2:255) is the greatest ayah in the Quran. The Prophet (PBUH) said reciting it after every prayer leads to Jannah.
[quran] Surah Al-Mulk protects from the punishment of the grave. The Prophet (PBUH) recommended reciting it every night before sleep.

## hadith

[hadith] "The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it." — Prophet Muhammad (PBUH), Sahih al-Bukhari.
[hadith] "Actions are judged by intentions." First hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari — the foundation of all deeds in Islam.
[hadith] "Whoever treads a path seeking knowledge, Allah makes the path to Jannah easy for him." — Sahih Muslim.

## dua

[dua] Rabbi zidni ilma — "My Lord, increase me in knowledge." (Quran 20:114) A short but powerful dua to say before any learning session.
[dua] Bismillahi tawakkaltu ala Allah — say it when leaving home. The Prophet (PBUH) said you will be guided and protected.

## seerah

[seerah] The Prophet (PBUH) received the first revelation at age 40 in the Cave of Hira. The first word revealed was "Iqra" — Read.
[seerah] The Hijrah to Madinah in 622 CE marks the start of the Islamic calendar. It was a turning point for the Muslim community.

## ancient

[ancient] The Library of Alexandria held an estimated 400,000 scrolls. Scholars came from across the ancient world to study there.
[ancient] The Great Pyramid of Giza was the tallest man-made structure for over 3,800 years — from 2560 BC until Lincoln Cathedral in 1311 AD.

## modern

[modern] The printing press (1440) made books 80% cheaper within 50 years. It sparked the Reformation, the Scientific Revolution, and mass literacy.
[modern] Alan Turing's work breaking the Enigma code shortened WWII by an estimated 2 years and saved millions of lives.

## civilizations

[civilizations] The Islamic Golden Age (8th-14th century) gave us algebra, algorithms, optics, and hospitals. Baghdad's House of Wisdom was the world's intellectual center.
[civilizations] The Roman road network spanned 400,000 km. Some roads built 2,000 years ago are still in use today across Europe.

## solar-system

[solar-system] Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has been raging for at least 350 years.
[solar-system] Saturn's density is so low it would float in water — if you could find a bathtub big enough.

## cosmos

[cosmos] A teaspoon of neutron star material weighs about 6 billion tons — roughly the weight of Mount Everest.
[cosmos] Light from the Sun takes 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth. When you see a sunset, it already happened 8 minutes ago.

## exploration

[exploration] Voyager 1 launched in 1977 is now over 24 billion km away — the farthest human-made object from Earth, still sending data.
[exploration] The ISS orbits Earth every 90 minutes at 28,000 km/h. Astronauts see 16 sunrises and sunsets every day.

## spacex

[spacex] Starship is the tallest and most powerful rocket ever built — 121 meters tall with 33 Raptor engines generating 74 MN of thrust at liftoff.
[spacex] SpaceX lands Falcon 9 boosters on drone ships at sea. Over 300 successful landings, making rockets reusable for the first time in history.
[spacex] Raptor engines use a full-flow staged combustion cycle — both fuel and oxidizer are fully gasified before entering the combustion chamber.

## starlink

[starlink] Starlink satellites use laser links to bounce data between satellites in orbit, reducing latency for remote and ocean coverage.
[starlink] Over 6,000 Starlink satellites orbit Earth, making it the largest satellite constellation ever deployed.

## nasa

[nasa] The James Webb Space Telescope orbits the Sun at L2, 1.5 million km from Earth. Its mirror is 6.5m wide and sees infrared light from the first galaxies.
[nasa] Voyager 1 launched in 1977 is now over 24 billion km away — the farthest human-made object, still sending data back to Earth.
[nasa] The Artemis program aims to land the first woman and first person of color on the Moon, using Orion and a SpaceX Starship lunar lander.

## moon

[moon] Apollo 17 in 1972 was the last crewed Moon mission. No human has been beyond low Earth orbit since — over 50 years ago.
[moon] NASA confirmed water ice exists in permanently shadowed craters at the Moon's south pole. Future bases could mine it for drinking water and rocket fuel.

## rockets

[rockets] Rocket staging works because dropping empty tanks mid-flight means less mass to accelerate — each stage gets a better thrust-to-weight ratio.
[rockets] The Tsiolkovsky rocket equation shows that most of a rocket's mass must be propellant. A typical rocket is ~90% fuel by weight at launch.
